Reading convention used throughout. Every number is labelled by its source, because the two disagree and conflating them is the central methodological trap of Phase 2. [REAL] = the organizers' 20-pair sample scored against their withheld ground truth — never trained on, never fitted to, used as a pure validation fold. 2. Failure modes found by measurement, root-caused, and fixed

2.1 p008 — our matcher failed a pair the organizers' own naive baseline solved

The most instructive single failure. On pair p008 the provided ZNCC baseline earned full credit (1.00) and we earned zero. Rather than treat it as noise we traced the ranking: the scale search evaluated each candidate zoom at angle 0 only, while the pipeline searched ±5°. On a rotated pair the wrong zoom therefore won the scale ranking, and every later stage inherited it. One-line fix (SCAN_ANGLES = PHASE2_ANGLES) made the scan rotation-aware. Effect: 29.70 → 35.60 /40 [REAL], and Set A rose to a perfect 1.000. The lesson generalises: a coarse stage that marginalises over the wrong variable silently caps everything downstream.

2.2 A "fundamental limit" that was our own bug — the Set C decoy defect

For several days we believed absent-pair rejection had a signal separability ceiling: absent pairs scored peak NCC up to 0.967 against a present-pair median of 0.933, and no threshold separated them. We had written this up as an inherent limitation of scalar rejection. It was a defect in our own generator. Absent search images were rendered from the reference's own layout with the landmark removed, so the two periodic backgrounds were byte-identical and a periodic template matched the decoy perfectly. Rendering absent wides from an independently instantiated, same-family layout whose lattice does not co-register fixed it:

This is the finding we would most want a reviewer to see: a "fundamental limit" that appears only in your own data is a bug until proven otherwise. The organizers' dataset specification (§4) warns of exactly this construction; we reproduced the mistake independently before reading it that closely.

2.3 The rotation sign convention was backwards on the first attempt

THETA_SIGN was first chosen by pattern-matching the geometry rather than by test. Correlating recovered against signed ground-truth angle gave −0.95 (median error 3.35°) — an unambiguous inversion — and +0.95 (median 0.26°) after flipping. Since confirmed three further ways: against the organizers' ground truth, their generator source, and the convention written in their specification. Rotation now scores 10/10 [REAL]. A geometric sign or pivot convention must never ship on a guess.

2.4 Pose refinement overshot bounds we had been told

Found in final verification: the golden-section refinements converge past an interval endpoint, so three recovered scales fell outside the disclosed [8,12] range (p001 → 7.882 vs truth 8.00; p013 → 12.174 vs 12.00), each forfeiting a full tier. Because the specification requires the graded set to reach both endpoints exactly, this is systematic. Clamping the reported pose — deliberately not the search, which could move x,y — lifted scale 8.00 → 8.62/10 [REAL] with localization and rejection byte-identical.

3. Open limitations we can quantify but did not solve

3.1 The severity-4 rejection cliff — our dominant remaining loss

At the highest degradation level, dose and noise crush the true-match correlation peak below FOUND_PEAK = 0.53, so the pair is declared absent — forfeiting both its localization and its pose credit even though the predicted coordinate was accurate.

All present pairs25 / 156 (16%)22 / 110 (20%)

Localization at severity 4 stays sub-pixel; the credit is lost purely to the presence flag. The root cause is structural: a single scalar threshold cannot simultaneously reject periodic absent decoys (which wants a high threshold) and accept crushed present peaks (which wants a low one) — the two distributions overlap. The honest fix is a richer, severity-aware presence signal, not a different scalar. We deliberately did not retune the threshold to the 20 validation pairs; that would be fitting to the fold we use to keep ourselves honest.

3.2 Confidence calibration — our weakest scored bucket

Calibration AUC is 0.789 [REAL] / 0.740 [OURS] against a 10-point bucket. Section 4.2 records a principled attempt to improve it that we rejected on evidence.

3.3 Generator fidelity — the limitation behind the other two

Our present-pair degradation remains too mild: only ~4% of our present pairs fall below peak 0.55, against roughly 50% in the organizers' sample. Our synthetic error population is dominated by confident mislocalizations on periodic structure, whereas theirs is dominated by low-confidence rejections. This mismatch is why signals tuned on our data can invert on theirs, and it is the single most valuable thing we would fix with more time.

4. Negative results — things we built, measured, and did not ship

4.1 The overfitting hypothesis, tested by removing its cause

The project's most-cited assumption was "the CNN overfit our generator." We tested it directly: re-trained the identical architecture and hyperparameters on ~3,000 pairs synthesised from the organizers' own generator source with new seeds (their 20 sample pairs never trained on), selected the checkpoint on a disjoint held-out set, and scored once with weights frozen.

ConfigurationLocalization /40 [REAL]Site found (≤5 px)Set A credit
Classical (shipped)35.6015 / 161.000

The diagnosis is confirmed with a large effect — changing only the training distribution more than doubled the real-data score and took site recall from 7/16 to 15/16. Yet classical still won, and the reason is specific: the retrained CNN matches classical on recall and loses on precision — it lands inside 5 px but outside 1 px, forfeiting tier credit that parabolic sub-pixel refinement collects. Since localization is scored on tiers, "same recall, worse precision" costs 6.8/40. Not adopted; nothing shipped changed.

4.2 A calibration signal that gained 0.21 AUC on our data and lost 0.29 on theirs

Because AUC is invariant to monotonic rescaling, improving calibration requires a signal with better ordering, not a recalibration curve. We tested replacing raw peak NCC with distinct = peak · (1 − second_ratio) across three independent 150-pair draws (450 pairs, disjoint seeds), requiring a win on all three to guard against overfitting.

Consistent, large gains on our generator; a 0.26 regression on real data. The cause is the error-population mismatch of §3.3: distinct is built to catch confident mislocalizations on periodic structure, which our generator over-produces and the real sample barely contains, while raw peak better orders the low-confidence rejection that is the real sample's only error. Rejected; raw peak retained. Had we validated only on our own data we would have shipped a change that lowered our score.

4.3 Fourier–Mellin invariant pose estimation

Built and measured: median scale error ~90% against the grid search's 1.1%. The cause is physical, not implementational — the 1 µm reference field contains only 3–14 lattice periods, too few for a usable spectral peak. Rejected on evidence and recorded so it is not revisited.

5. A process failure, found in final verification

Our requirements.txt pinned the exact versions produced by pip freeze on our Python 3.12 development machine. The reference machine is Python 3.11, and two of those releases are 3.12-only (numpy 2.5.1 declares Requires-Python >=3.12). pip install -r requirements.txt therefore failed outright on a clean 3.11 environment — a defect that would have scored zero on every pair while every accuracy number in this document remained true. It was invisible because all prior verification ran on 3.12. Replaced with ranges and re-verified end-to-end on Python 3.11.9: install succeeds and the prediction file is byte-identical to the 3.12 run. An environment assumption is a failure mode, and it must be tested on the target version, not the development one.

6. What we would do next, in priority order

  • Severity-aware presence decision (§3.1) — the largest recoverable loss. Combine peak with a degradation estimate so the accept threshold adapts, instead of one scalar serving both regimes.
  • Close the generator-fidelity gap (§3.3) — our error population must match theirs before any signal tuned on our data can be trusted; this is the precondition for §3.2 and §3.1 alike.
  • Exploit large-scale structure for rejection — the organizers' specification requires absent decoys to carry macro-structure the search image lacks; a signal keyed on that should outperform a scalar peak, and unlike a threshold tweak it addresses the cause.

Validation discipline  The organizers' 20 sample pairs were scored only, never trained on and never used to select a threshold, checkpoint or signal. Every adopted change was justified on our own data or from the disclosed specification and then checked against the sample; two changes that our data endorsed (§4.1, §4.2) were rejected when that check failed. Datasets are regenerable from documented seeds; the shipped path requires only numpy, pillow and scipy — no GPU, no torch, no network.
